# Access Paths: Finding Anything

### The Decision Tree

**Step 1: Does it exist in open access?**
- Google Scholar: look for [PDF] links on the right
- Unpaywall browser extension: automatically surfaces 
  legal free versions. Install this immediately.
- Open Access Button: openaccessbutton.org
- Author's page: search "[Author Name] [Title] PDF"
- ResearchGate: authors often upload their own papers
- PubMed Central: US National Library of Medicine, 
  extensive free access to biomedical research

**Step 2: Does your library have it?**
- University library: if you have any affiliation, 
  use it. Alumni access is often maintained.
- Public library: more powerful than most people 
  know. Libby/OverDrive for ebooks and audiobooks. 
  Kanopy for documentaries and films.

**Step 3: Interlibrary Loan (ILL)**
Any book or article in any library worldwide can be 
requested through ILL. Often free, though some systems 
charge $3-25 per request. Rural and Global South 
libraries may have limited ILL infrastructure. 
Usually takes 2-7 days. Criminally underused.
Ask at any library reference desk.

**Step 4: Direct to author**
Email the author. Response rate is 60-80% for 
academic papers. Template:
"Dear Professor [Name], I am researching [topic] 
and would be grateful for a copy of your paper 
[Title]. Thank you."

For books: the same approach works. Authors are 
often willing to share chapters or preprint versions, 
especially for researchers and students.

**Step 5: Preprint servers**
For recent research, check arXiv, bioRxiv, 
medRxiv, SSRN, PhilPapers, PsyArXiv, EarthArXiv 
before assuming a paper is unavailable.

**Step 6: Shadow libraries (legal status varies)**
- Anna's Archive (annas-archive.org): 
  the largest index. Start here.
- Sci-Hub: journal articles by DOI. Mirror URLs 
  rotate frequently; search "Sci-Hub working mirror" 
  to find current addresses.
- Library Genesis (libgen.is and mirrors): 
  books and papers
- Z-Library (various mirrors): books

Legal note: accessing these varies in legality 
by jurisdiction. In many countries with limited 
academic access, these are the only viable 
paths to research.

### Finding Books Specifically

**New books at lowest cost**
- ThriftBooks: often $4-8 including shipping
- AbeBooks: out-of-print and used
- Bookfinder.com: aggregates prices across vendors
- WorldCat (worldcat.org): locate nearest library copy
- Better World Books: used, funds literacy programs

**Out of print and rare**
- AbeBooks
- Alibris
- VIALibri: specialized rare book search
- eBay (underrated for academic books)
- WorldCat for library copies

**Digital books legally free**
- Project Gutenberg: 70,000+ public domain texts
- Standard Ebooks: public domain with professional 
  formatting
- Open Library (archive.org/openlibrary): 
  controlled digital lending
- HathiTrust: millions of scanned volumes, 
  full text for public domain works
- Google Books: partial preview for many 
  in-copyright works

### Finding Papers Specifically

**By DOI**
- Paste into doi.org/[DOI] for official version

**By topic**
- Google Scholar: broadest coverage
- Semantic Scholar: AI-enhanced, shows influence 
  and related papers
- PubMed: biomedical focus
- Web of Science: citation tracking
- Scopus: citation tracking
- Domain-specific databases (see domain maps)

**By citation network**
- Connected Papers (connectedpapers.com): 
  visual network around any paper
- Litmaps (litmaps.com): timeline view
- Citation Gecko: builds reading list from seeds
- Inciteful: similar papers and citation paths

### Finding Datasets and Code

**General research data**
- Zenodo (zenodo.org): open repository for 
  research data, software, and publications
- Figshare: research data and figures
- Open Science Framework (osf.io): 
  preregistrations, data, code, and materials
- Harvard Dataverse: broad disciplinary coverage
- re3data.org: registry of research data 
  repositories by discipline

**Social and behavioral sciences**
- ICPSR (icpsr.umich.edu): the largest archive 
  of social science data
- UK Data Service: UK and international datasets
- Roper Center: public opinion data

**Health and biomedical**
- NCBI databases: genomic and biomedical data
- PhysioNet: physiological data
- NIMH Data Archive: mental health research data

**Code and software**
- GitHub / GitLab: code repositories
- Software Heritage (softwareheritage.org): 
  long-term preservation of source code
- Papers with Code: ML papers with implementations
- protocols.io: reproducible laboratory protocols

**Government and economic data**
- World Bank Open Data: data.worldbank.org
- OECD Data: data.oecd.org
- US Census Bureau: census.gov/data
- Eurostat: ec.europa.eu/eurostat
- FRED (Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis): 
  economic time series

### Finding Grey Literature

**Government and policy**
- Google search: site:gov [topic]
- Congressional Research Service: 
  everycrsreport.com (now public)
- RAND Corporation: rand.org/pubs
- Brookings: brookings.edu
- Pew Research: pewresearch.org
- NBER Working Papers: nber.org/papers

**International organizations**
- World Bank Open Knowledge: 
  openknowledge.worldbank.org
- IMF Publications: imf.org/publications
- OECD iLibrary: much free access
- UN Documents: undocs.org

**Dissertations**
- ProQuest Dissertations: partial free access
- EThOS: British Library UK theses, many free
- DART-Europe: European dissertations
- PQDT Open: open access dissertations

### Finding Historical and Primary Sources

**Archives and special collections**
- Internet Archive (archive.org)
- HathiTrust: scanned books with full text
- JSTOR's Early Journal Content: 
  articles before 1923, free
- Europeana: European cultural heritage
- Digital Public Library of America
- Gallica: Bibliothèque nationale de France
- British Library digitized collections

**Declassified government documents**
- National Security Archive: nsarchive.gwu.edu
- CIA CREST: cia.gov/readingroom
- FOIA.gov: US Freedom of Information requests

**Newspapers and periodicals historically**
- Chronicling America: US newspapers 1770-1963, free
- British Newspaper Archive: paid
- ProQuest Historical Newspapers: via library

---

### Finding Non-English Sources

**Translation quality**
Translation quality matters enormously for philosophy, 
literature, and history. Always note which translation 
is being used. For major works, a definitive translation 
is usually identifiable; ask a domain expert if unsure.

**Non-English databases**
- Persée: French academic journals, free
- DigiZeitschriften: German periodicals
- JSTOR (increasingly international coverage)
- Redalyc: Latin American journals, open access
- SciELO: Brazilian and Latin American science
- CiNii: Japanese academic papers
- CNKI: Chinese academic papers (paid access)
- KCI: Korean academic journals

**Finding non-English books**
- WorldCat searches globally
- Karlsruher Virtueller Katalog: aggregates 
  European library catalogs
- Bibliothèque nationale de France catalog
- Deutsche Nationalbibliothek catalog
