# Philosophy: Intellectual Topology

### The Major Traditions

**Ancient and Classical (600 BCE to 500 CE)**
Origin points for almost all Western philosophical problems.
- Pre-Socratics: Heraclitus, Parmenides, Democritus. The 
  original questions about change, being, and matter.
- Plato: The dialogues are the scaffolding of Western thought. 
  Start with Meno, then Republic, then Phaedrus, then 
  Theaetetus.
- Aristotle: More important than Plato for science, biology, 
  logic, ethics, and rhetoric. The Nicomachean Ethics and 
  Poetics remain essential.
- Stoics: Epictetus, Marcus Aurelius, Seneca. Underrated as 
  philosophy; overrated as self-help. Read the Discourses 
  before Meditations.
- Epicureans: Lucretius' De Rerum Natura is the primary text. 
  Everything else is fragments.
- Neoplatonists: Plotinus' Enneads. Crucial bridge between 
  ancient philosophy and medieval theology.

**Medieval (500 to 1400 CE)**
Systematically underread outside theology departments.
- Islamic Golden Age: Al-Kindi, Al-Farabi, Avicenna (Ibn 
  Sina), Averroes (Ibn Rushd). Preserved and extended Greek 
  philosophy while Europe was in collapse.
- Jewish medieval philosophy: Maimonides' Guide for the 
  Perplexed is essential.
- Scholasticism: Aquinas' Summa Theologica.
- William of Ockham: Nominalism and the origins of 
  empiricism. Ockham's razor is a philosophical position, 
  not just a heuristic.

**Early Modern (1500 to 1800)**
- Descartes: Meditations on First Philosophy.
- Spinoza: Ethics. Underread.
- Leibniz: Monadology and the correspondence with Clarke.
- Locke: Essay Concerning Human Understanding.
- Hume: Enquiries and Treatise of Human Nature.
- Kant: Critique of Pure Reason (hard), then Groundwork for 
  the Metaphysics of Morals. Read Strawson's Bounds of Sense 
  as a companion.

**19th Century**
- Hegel: Phenomenology of Spirit. Read Kojeve's Introduction 
  to the Reading of Hegel first.
- Marx: Economic and Philosophic Manuscripts of 1844 before 
  Capital.
- Nietzsche: Beyond Good and Evil, then Genealogy of Morals. 
  Do not start with Thus Spoke Zarathustra.
- Kierkegaard: Either/Or and Fear and Trembling.
- Mill: On Liberty and Utilitarianism.
- Schopenhauer: The World as Will and Representation. 
  Directly influenced Nietzsche, Wittgenstein, and Freud.

**20th Century: Analytic Tradition**
- Frege: Foundations of Arithmetic.
- Russell and Whitehead: Principia Mathematica.
- Early Wittgenstein: Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus.
- Later Wittgenstein: Philosophical Investigations. A complete 
  reversal. Both are essential.
- Logical Positivists: Vienna Circle, Carnap, Ayer.
- Quine: "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" (essay).
- Rawls: A Theory of Justice.
- Kripke: Naming and Necessity.
- Parfit: Reasons and Persons.

**20th Century: Continental Tradition**
- Husserl: Ideas I. Foundation of phenomenology.
- Heidegger: Being and Time. Read Dreyfus' commentary 
  alongside.
- Sartre: Being and Nothingness, then Existentialism is a 
  Humanism for accessibility.
- Merleau-Ponty: Phenomenology of Perception.
- Foucault: Discipline and Punish, then The Order of Things.
- Derrida: Of Grammatology. Try Jonathan Culler's On 
  Deconstruction first.
- Deleuze: Anti-Oedipus (with Guattari) and A Thousand 
  Plateaus.
- Habermas: The Theory of Communicative Action.

**Philosophy of Science**
Essential for evaluating any empirical claim.
- Popper: The Logic of Scientific Discovery.
- Kuhn: The Structure of Scientific Revolutions. Paradigm 
  shifts. Among the most widely cited academic books of the 
  20th century across multiple disciplines.
- Lakatos: The Methodology of Scientific Research Programmes.
- Feyerabend: Against Method.
- van Fraassen: The Scientific Image.
- Hacking: Representing and Intervening.
- Longino: Science as Social Knowledge.

**Ethics: Major Frameworks**
- Consequentialism: Mill, Sidgwick, Singer
- Deontology: Kant, Ross, Scanlon
- Virtue Ethics: Aristotle, MacIntyre, Foot, Annas
- Contractualism: Rawls, Scanlon
- Care Ethics: Gilligan, Noddings, Held
- Moral Particularism: Dancy

**Political Philosophy**
- Liberalism: Locke, Mill, Rawls, Dworkin
- Communitarianism: MacIntyre, Sandel, Walzer, Taylor
- Republicanism: Pettit, Skinner
- Libertarianism: Nozick, Hayek
- Marxism: Marx, Gramsci, Cohen, Roemer
- Feminist: Wollstonecraft, de Beauvoir, Pateman, Young
- Postcolonial: Fanon, Spivak, Wynter, Mbembe

### Key Journals
- Mind (Oxford): analytic, established 1876
- Philosophical Review (Cornell)
- Ethics (Chicago): moral and political
- Philosophy and Public Affairs
- Journal of Philosophy
- Synthese: logic, philosophy of science
- European Journal of Philosophy
- Hypatia: feminist philosophy
- Philosophy East and West: non-Western traditions

### Key Databases
- PhilPapers (philpapers.org): the essential philosophy 
  index. Free. Best starting point for any topic.
- PhilArchive: open access philosophy papers

### Shadow Curriculum

- Bernard Williams: Ethics and the Limits of Philosophy
- G.E.M. Anscombe: Modern Moral Philosophy (essay)
- Wilfrid Sellars: Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind
- Nelson Goodman: Fact, Fiction and Forecast

  Practical Reason

### Non-Western Traditions
Coverage here is introductory. Each tradition warrants 
its own dedicated map. This skill's current coverage 
skews Western; acknowledge this explicitly when relevant.

**Indian Philosophy**
- Hindu: Upanishads, Bhagavad Gita, the six darshanas
- Buddhist: Pali Canon, Nagarjuna's Mulamadhyamakakarika
- Secondary: Matilal's Perception, Radhakrishnan's 
  Indian Philosophy (2 vols)

**Chinese Philosophy**
- Confucian: Analects, Mencius, Xunzi
- Daoist: Daodejing, Zhuangzi
- Neo-Confucian: Wang Yangming's Instructions for 
  Practical Living
- Secondary: Hansen's A Daoist Theory of Chinese Thought

**African Philosophy**
- Wiredu: Cultural Universals and Particulars
- Gyekye: An Essay on African Philosophical Thought
- Mbiti: African Religions and Philosophy

**Islamic Philosophy**
- Avicenna (Ibn Sina): The Book of Healing
- Al-Ghazali: The Incoherence of the Philosophers
- Averroes (Ibn Rushd): The Incoherence of the Incoherence
- Ibn Khaldun: Muqaddimah

### Cross-Domain Bridges
- Philosophy of mind to cognitive science: 
  Chalmers, Dennett, Clark
- Ethics to economics via decision theory: 
  Sen, Parfit, Nozick
- Political philosophy to law: Dworkin, Hart, Raz
- Philosophy of language to linguistics: 
  Chomsky, Austin, Grice
- Philosophy of science to history of science: 
  Kuhn, Daston, Hacking
