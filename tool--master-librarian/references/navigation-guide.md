# Navigation Guide: Information Layers and Cross-Domain Bridges

## The Six Information Layers

The ecosystem has six layers. Knowing which layer to search determines whether you find what you need in minutes or hours — and whether you find it at all.

| Layer | What lives here | Key tools |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Visible Library | Major databases, discovery tools, library catalogs | JSTOR, PubMed, Google Scholar, Semantic Scholar, WorldCat |
| 2. Open Access | Preprint servers, OA journals, author sharing | arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN, Unpaywall, ResearchGate |
| 3. Free Legitimate | Public domain, digital lending, ILL | Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg, HathiTrust, Libby |
| 4. Shadow Libraries | Legal gray area, widely used where institutional access unavailable | Anna's Archive, Library Genesis, Sci-Hub |
| 5. Deep/Specialized Web | Government repos, dissertations, standards, datasets | ERIC, RAND, PACER, Zenodo, ICPSR, OSF |
| 6. Emerging Knowledge | Preprint trends, forthcoming titles, active research groups | Preprint volume tracking, press catalogs |

---

## Layer-by-Layer Detail

### Layer 1 — Visible Library (Major Databases and Discovery Tools)

What lives here: Published, peer-reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings, and indexed books. The authoritative record of established scholarship.

Key tools and what they cover:
- **Google Scholar** — broadest index across disciplines; picks up grey literature, theses, and many preprints in addition to journals. Best starting point for unfamiliar territory.
- **Semantic Scholar** — AI-powered; surfaces citation context and influential papers. Better than Google Scholar for identifying foundational works by citation weight.
- **JSTOR** — stable, archival access to humanities, social science, and law journals. Strong for pre-2000 material.
- **PubMed / MEDLINE** — biomedical and clinical literature. Controlled vocabulary (MeSH terms) makes searches more precise than free-text.
- **Web of Science / Scopus** — citation tracking, journal impact metrics, institutional access required. Use for tracing who cited whom and mapping citation networks forward in time.
- **WorldCat** — global library union catalog. Use to find physical holdings and request ILL.
- **IEEE Xplore / ACM Digital Library** — engineering, computing, and technology conference proceedings. Conference papers often lead journals by 12–18 months in fast-moving fields.

When to start here: Always, unless the question is about very recent work (< 12 months) or the operator has confirmed they need open access only.

Layer 1 gap: Paywall access required for most content unless the operator has institutional access. If they don't, move to Layer 2 or 3 before reaching Layer 4.

---

### Layer 2 — Open Access (Preprints and Author Sharing)

What lives here: Preprints (not yet peer-reviewed), open-access journal versions, author self-archived copies, and working papers. Fast, free, and increasingly authoritative in fast-moving fields.

Key tools:
- **arXiv** — physics, mathematics, computer science, economics, statistics, quantitative biology. Preprints post within days; often months ahead of journal publication.
- **bioRxiv / medRxiv** — biology and medicine preprints. Quality varies; major findings often appear here before peer review.
- **SSRN** — social science and economics working papers. Covers law, finance, political science, management.
- **Unpaywall** — browser extension that finds legal free versions of paywalled papers. Run this before assuming a paper is inaccessible.
- **ResearchGate** — author profiles and self-shared PDFs. Quality varies by field and author. Legitimate when the author posted their own work.
- **PhilPapers** — philosophy preprints and published works. Discipline-specific.

When to use Layer 2: (a) When a paper found in Layer 1 is paywalled and Unpaywall finds a free version. (b) When searching for very recent work in fast-moving empirical fields — arXiv and bioRxiv will have it before journals do. (c) When looking for working papers or early-stage research before formal publication.

Layer 2 gap: Preprints are not peer-reviewed. Flag this when recommending Layer 2 sources. Do not present a preprint as having the same epistemic status as a published, replicated finding.

---

### Layer 3 — Free Legitimate (Public Domain and Digital Lending)

What lives here: Books and articles in the public domain, digitally lent library books, and ILL (Interlibrary Loan).

Key tools:
- **Internet Archive / Open Library** — scans of public domain books, plus digital lending of in-copyright books for registered users. Coverage is broad but quality varies.
- **Project Gutenberg** — clean plain-text versions of public domain literary and philosophical works. Reliable for pre-1928 texts.
- **HathiTrust** — university library consortium with millions of scanned volumes. Full text for public domain works; page-limited views for in-copyright material.
- **Libby / OverDrive** — ebook and audiobook borrowing via public library card. Surprisingly good coverage for recent popular and academic titles.
- **Interlibrary Loan (ILL)** — library-to-library lending; almost any book or article can be obtained this way given time. Free with a library card. Turnaround: 1–10 days.

When to use Layer 3: When Layer 1 and 2 fail to produce a free version, and the operator has a library card or is willing to wait for ILL. ILL is the cleanest, fully legal option for paywalled academic content.

Layer 3 gap: ILL takes time. For historical texts, Open Library scans may be imperfect.

---

### Layer 4 — Shadow Libraries (Legal Gray Area)

What lives here: Pirated copies of journal articles and books, uploaded without publisher consent. Widely used in academic circles, especially in countries without institutional access. Legal status varies by jurisdiction — generally not permitted under copyright law in the US/EU, but largely tolerated in practice.

Key tools:
- **Anna's Archive** — aggregator that searches Library Genesis, Sci-Hub, and other sources in one interface. Best first stop if using this layer.
- **Library Genesis (Libgen)** — large collection of books and articles. Coverage is broad; quality varies.
- **Sci-Hub** — journal article access by DOI. High coverage for major journal publishers.

When to surface Layer 4: Only after Layers 1–3 are exhausted. Surface it honestly: "This is available via shadow libraries if legitimate access has failed; legal status varies by jurisdiction." Do not be evasive about what these tools are. Do not proactively recommend them before legitimate access paths have been tried.

Layer 4 gap: Publishers occasionally take down mirror sites. URLs change. Citation verification is harder since metadata is not always reliable.

---

### Layer 5 — Deep/Specialized Web (Government, Datasets, Grey Literature)

What lives here: Government reports, policy documents, technical standards, court records, dissertations, and research datasets. Underused and undervalued.

Key tools:
- **ERIC** — education research, including grey literature and government reports.
- **RAND Corporation** — defense, policy, and public health research reports. Free access.
- **PACER** — US federal court documents and filings.
- **Zenodo** — research data, software, and supplementary materials for published papers. Operated by CERN.
- **ICPSR** — social science and political science datasets. University of Michigan.
- **OSF (Open Science Framework)** — preprints, data, and replication materials for psychology and adjacent fields.
- **Government portals** — data.gov, OECD iLibrary, Eurostat, national statistical agencies. For statistics, policy documents, and official reports.
- **ProQuest Dissertations** — the primary source for unpublished PhD theses. Many fields have important work that never appeared in journals.

When to use Layer 5: When the question is empirical and the best evidence is in government data or research datasets. When tracking policy or regulation. When looking for dissertation-level deep dives on niche topics not covered in published journals. When supplementary materials (datasets, code) for a published paper are needed.

Layer 5 gap: Highly heterogeneous — no single search strategy covers this layer. Navigation is domain-specific.

---

### Layer 6 — Emerging Knowledge (Frontiers and Forthcoming)

What lives here: Research that is not yet published or widely cited — preprint volume signals, forthcoming books from major university presses, conference proceedings, active research group output.

Navigation strategy:
- **Preprint volume** — search arXiv, bioRxiv, or SSRN for the last 12–24 months. High volume clustering around a term = active frontier. Thin results = mature or stagnant field.
- **Forthcoming titles** — check Oxford, Cambridge, MIT, Princeton, Chicago press online catalogs for announced books. These represent what the field's establishment considers the next important contribution.
- **Conference proceedings** — in fast-moving empirical fields (ML, medicine, economics), conference papers lead journals by 12–18 months. Check NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR (AI/ML), ASA (statistics), AEA (economics), etc.
- **Active research groups** — identify the 3–5 groups producing the most-cited recent work. Track their lab pages, working paper series, and forthcoming sections.

When to use Layer 6: When the operator needs a frontier scan, not just established literature. Always pair Layer 6 output with a knowledge-cutoff disclosure: the skill's training has a cutoff, and Layer 6 is the layer most likely to be stale.

## How to Decide Which Layer to Consult First

Use this decision tree for any query:

1. **Is the source already known and the question is just access?** → Start at Layer 1 (Unpaywall), then Layer 2 (author sharing), then Layer 3 (ILL), then Layer 4 (shadow libraries as last resort).

2. **Is the operator looking for established foundational work?** → Layer 1 first. Use Semantic Scholar or Google Scholar for citation-weighted discovery.

3. **Is the field moving faster than 12 months?** → Layer 2 (arXiv, bioRxiv, SSRN) before Layer 1. Journals will lag.

4. **Is the question about policy, government data, or regulation?** → Layer 5 first. Government portals and grey literature often hold the most direct evidence.

5. **Is the question about what is coming next in a field?** → Layer 6. Preprint volume + press catalogs + conference tracks.

6. **Is the topic a niche subfield or an interdisciplinary intersection?** → Layer 5 (dissertations, working papers) plus cross-domain bridges (see below).

Always state which layer you are searching and why. The operator needs to understand the evidence tier of what you are finding.

## Cross-Domain Bridges

When a question in one domain is better answered by literature in another — name the bridge explicitly. Researchers often miss the best evidence because they search only their home field's literature.

| Question domain | Look first in | Common miss |
|---|---|---|
| Organizational behavior | Sociology and anthropology | Business literature often re-invents findings from Goffman, Bourdieu, etc. |
| Decision-making | Behavioral economics and cognitive science | Management literature often lacks the mechanistic precision of the psychological research |
| Communication strategy | Rhetoric, linguistics, and semiotics | Marketing literature skips 2,500 years of persuasion theory |
| Technology impact | Philosophy and sociology of technology | Technologists underread Langdon Winner, Judy Wajcman, Shoshana Zuboff |
| Policy design | Law reviews and political science | News media is not a substitute for primary legislative and judicial records |
| Measurement and assessment | Psychometrics and statistics | Domain fields often use bespoke instruments while ignoring IRT or Rasch literature |
| Innovation and diffusion | Economic history and sociology of science | "Innovation" frameworks often miss Schumpeter, Rogers, and the sociology of scientific knowledge |
| Health behavior | Social psychology and medical anthropology | Clinical literature often misses the cultural and structural determinants of behavior |

**How to apply a bridge:** When a reading curriculum in domain X has a gap, ask: "What other field has studied this mechanism directly?" Name the bridge explicitly to the operator. Example: "The organizational dynamics you are asking about are well-studied in sociology under institutional theory — the business management literature on this is downstream of Selznick and DiMaggio & Powell."
