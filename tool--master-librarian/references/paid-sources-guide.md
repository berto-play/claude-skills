# Paid Source Landscape & Cost-Value Framework

Reference for Workflow 2 step 5 and Workflow 7. Not everything worth having is free — the librarian's job is to say when paying is rational, not to pretend the free tier always suffices.

**Price rule (hard):** never state a current price from training knowledge. Prices here are indicative ranges as of the skill's knowledge cutoff, marked as such. Before any purchase recommendation: verify live if tools exist, otherwise instruct the operator to check the vendor page. A wrong price in a cost-value verdict invalidates the verdict.

---

## The cost-value check (run before recommending any paid source)

1. **Coverage**: What fraction of the operator's actual need does this unlock? Name it ("~80% of the corpus spec's critical tier").
2. **Free-tier residual**: What of that coverage is already reachable free (open access, library, ILL, deep web)? Paid is justified by the *residual*, not the headline coverage.
3. **Cost shape**: One-off purchase vs. subscription vs. metered API. Subscriptions need a cancellation trigger ("cancel after corpus acquisition completes").
4. **Alternatives**: Is there an institutional route (university alumni access, public-library database portals, national-library cards) that gets the same content at zero cost? Check before recommending spend.
5. **Verdict format**: "Pay / Don't pay / Pay briefly" + the residual it buys + the trigger to stop paying.

## Paid landscape by category (indicative, verify current pricing)

| Category | Representative sources | Cost shape | When it beats free |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Academic journal access** | JSTOR (JPASS), individual publisher passes | Subscription, ~$20/mo range historically | Humanities/social-science back-catalogs; residual after Unpaywall + ILL is still large |
| **Citation/abstract databases** | Scopus, Web of Science | Institutional pricing, rarely individual | Almost never for individuals — use OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar (free) first |
| **Market/industry data** | Statista, IBISWorld, Gartner | Subscription, steep | Only when a deliverable needs citable market numbers; check public-library portals first |
| **News archives** | Nexis, Factiva, newspaper archives | Subscription/metered | Historical press research; many public libraries provide free portals |
| **Books** | Direct purchase, print or ebook | One-off, low | When ILL wait beats deadline, or the work is a permanent-reference tier source |
| **Datasets & APIs** | Commercial datasets, data APIs | One-off or metered | When the corpus spec has a critical data component with no open equivalent |
| **Dissertations & theses** | ProQuest individual purchase | One-off per document | Only after checking the university's own repository (often free) |

## Institutional side doors (always check before paying)

- **Public library database portals**: many libraries bundle JSTOR, Nexis-class news archives, Statista-class market data, and language/skill platforms with a free library card — including remote access.
- **University alumni access**: many universities grant alumni JSTOR/database subsets.
- **National library cards**: national libraries often grant residents remote access to licensed databases.
- **Author route**: for any single paper, emailing the author is free and usually works.

The cheapest paid source is the one an institution already bought for you.

## Red flags

- A paid source whose sample/preview can't be inspected before purchase.
- Subscriptions justified by "might need it later" — that is inventory, not acquisition (muda).
- Any vendor price quoted in this skill's output without a live check or an "indicative, verify" marker.
