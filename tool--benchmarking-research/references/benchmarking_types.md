# Benchmarking Types and Selection Framework

This reference guide details the different types of benchmarking and provides a framework for selecting the most appropriate type for a given exercise.

## Types of Benchmarking

Benchmarking can be categorized along two primary dimensions: **by subject matter** (what is being benchmarked) and **by comparator** (who you are benchmarking against).

### By Comparator

| Type | Description | Pros & Cons |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | Comparing performance across different units, teams, departments, or geographies within the same organization. | **Pros:** Data is readily accessible; definitions are consistent.<br>**Cons:** Cannot reveal how the organization performs relative to the outside world. |
| **Competitive** | Direct comparison against industry rivals. | **Pros:** Highly relevant.<br>**Cons:** Difficult to execute as competitors rarely share detailed operational data; relies on public or third-party data. |
| **Functional** | Comparing a specific function or process (e.g., procurement, HR) against organizations performing the same function, regardless of industry. | **Pros:** Broadens the pool of comparators; surfaces innovative practices.<br>**Cons:** Requires careful normalization. |
| **Generic / Best-in-Class** | Looking beyond industry boundaries entirely to identify organizations that are world-class at a particular process. | **Pros:** Yields transformational insights.<br>**Cons:** Most ambitious form; difficult to implement. |

### By Subject Matter

| Type | Focus Area | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Process** | How a specific process is executed | Order fulfillment cycle time |
| **Performance** | Quantitative output or outcome metrics | Cost per transaction, defect rate |
| **Strategic** | Long-term strategies, business models, and competitive positioning | Market entry strategies, portfolio decisions |
| **Product/Service** | Features, quality, and value of products or services | Product teardown analysis, customer satisfaction scores |
| **Financial** | Financial ratios and efficiency metrics | EBITDA margin, return on assets |
| **Digital/Technology** | Technology adoption, digital maturity, and IT capabilities | Cloud adoption rate, automation levels |

### Specialized Forms

- **Collaborative Benchmarking**: A group of organizations sharing data collectively, typically facilitated by a neutral third party.
- **Regulatory or Compliance Benchmarking**: Assessing whether an organization meets or exceeds regulatory standards or industry codes of practice.
- **Sustainability Benchmarking**: Measuring environmental, social, and governance (ESG) performance against peers or recognized frameworks.

## Selection Framework

Use the following sequential questions to guide the selection of the appropriate benchmarking type:

1. **Is the primary goal internal alignment or external comparison?**
   - Internal alignment → Internal Benchmarking
   - External comparison → Proceed to next question

2. **Do you need to understand *what* the gap is, or *how* to close it?**
   - *What* the gap is → Performance Benchmarking
   - *How* to close it → Process Benchmarking

3. **Is the answer likely to exist within your industry, or do you need to look further afield?**
   - Within industry → Competitive or Functional Benchmarking
   - Further afield → Generic / Best-in-Class Benchmarking

4. **Is data accessible at the required level of granularity?**
   - If not, reconsider the type or invest in data acquisition.

5. **What is the scale of change being targeted?**
   - Incremental improvement → Competitive Benchmarking
   - Transformational improvement → Generic / Best-in-Class Benchmarking

*Note: In many cases, the most robust exercises combine two types (e.g., competitive performance benchmarking to quantify the gap, and functional process benchmarking to understand how to close it).*
