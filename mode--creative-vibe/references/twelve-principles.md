# The Twelve Principles

Source: THE MANIFESTO OF BALANCED CREATION, `00 Shared Global Assets/Global Design Language/MANIFESTO.md`. This file is a working excerpt — if the source is reachable, read it directly for full text and lineage detail.

Each principle has a belief, a gate (a test that can fail), and a lineage. The gate is what makes this enforceable rather than aspirational.

## 1. Purpose Before Decoration
**Lineage:** Bauhaus, Dieter Rams.
**Belief:** Form follows function. Every creative decision must have a reason — improve understanding, strengthen emotion, clarify information, improve usability, reinforce identity. If none apply, remove it.
**Gate:** Name the job of every element out loud. Anything with no nameable job is cut.
**Fails when:** an element survives because it "looks better there," fills space, or was already in the file.

## 2. Structure Creates Freedom
**Lineage:** Josef Müller-Brockmann, Swiss International Typographic Style, Otl Aicher.
**Belief:** The grid is invisible architecture — a foundation, not a prison. Space is part of the structure, not what's left over after it.
**Gate:** Remove every border, box, background, and divider. Is the structure still legible?
**Fails when:** structure was being carried by decoration instead of alignment and space.

## 3. Reduction Reveals Truth
**Lineage:** Armin Hofmann.
**Belief:** The designer's job is identifying what matters. Remove unnecessary colors, decoration, redundant elements, and noise — not minimalism for its own sake, clarity.
**Gate:** Remove elements one at a time until meaning breaks. Restore the last one. That is the finished state.
**Fails when:** nothing was tried for removal, or removal stopped at the first uncomfortable moment rather than the first broken one.

## 4. Proportion Creates Harmony
**Lineage:** Max Bill, Concrete Art, Ulm School of Design.
**Belief:** Harmony is a relationship between differences, not sameness. Balance can come from large/small, light/dark, empty/full, static/dynamic.
**Gate:** Can you state the ratio or rule behind every size and every gap?
**Fails when:** any value exists because it "looked about right," or a scale has arbitrary steps that follow no system.

## 5. Typography Is the Voice of Structure
**Lineage:** Swiss typography, Jan Tschichold, Massimo Vignelli.
**Belief:** Typography is architecture, not styling. Communication is the first responsibility; personality is the second, in that order, always. Few families, distinct roles, never mixed.
**Gate:** Print it in a single color, all boxes and color removed. Does the eye still land in the right order?
**Fails when:** hierarchy was carried by color or containers, not size, weight, and space.

## 6. Simplicity Creates Recognition
**Lineage:** Paul Rand.
**Belief:** Simplicity is not removing intelligence — it is concentrating intelligence. A powerful identity communicates quickly, survives reduction, remains recognizable.
**Gate:** Describe the idea in one sentence, to someone who hasn't seen it. Then shrink the work to a favicon.
**Fails when:** the sentence needs a second sentence, or the mark stops being itself at small size.

## 7. Abstraction Creates New Understanding
**Lineage:** Cubist thinking (Picasso, Braque) — studied as a way of seeing, never reproduced as a style.
**Belief:** Design should reveal hidden relationships, not just represent what exists. This is the principle that keeps the other eleven from becoming a formula — discipline without exploration produces work that is correct and dead.
**Gate:** Name the viewpoint that was rejected, and why this one is better.
**Fails when:** only one approach was ever considered. The first idea is a starting point, not an answer.

## 8. Humanity Is the Center
**Lineage:** Bauhaus social purpose, Otl Aicher, Dieter Rams.
**Belief:** Technology is not the purpose, people are. Assume the person is distracted, in a hurry, tired, on a poor screen — design for that person and everyone else is covered. Accessibility is structural, not remedial.
**Gate:** Can a first-time user do the primary task without instruction? Does it hold at low vision, low attention, low bandwidth, low patience?
**Fails when:** understanding requires onboarding, or meaning is carried by color alone, motion alone, or hearing alone.

## 9. Materials Must Be Honest
**Lineage:** Bauhaus truth to materials, Dieter Rams.
**Belief:** Every medium has its own language — understand it, don't imitate another. Honesty extends to the message: no manufactured scarcity, no fake progress, no dark patterns, no decoration pretending to be data.
**Gate:** Would you be comfortable explaining every element to the person it's aimed at, out loud?
**Fails when:** any element persuades through misdirection, or any surface pretends to be a material it is not.

## 10. Restraint Creates Power
**Lineage:** Armin Hofmann, Dieter Rams, Kenya Hara.
**Belief:** Confidence does not require excess. One thing leads per view; everything else waits. No urgency the person didn't create.
**Gate:** Look at it for two seconds. Can you say what it wants you to do?
**Fails when:** two things answer. One of them is wrong.

## 11. Timelessness Over Trends
**Lineage:** Massimo Vignelli, Dieter Rams.
**Belief:** Trends are temporary expressions; principles endure. A trend is a solution borrowed without its problem. Using a current form is not a failure — using it *because* it is current is.
**Gate:** Name the reason for each stylistic choice without referencing what is currently popular.
**Fails when:** the only available justification is recency, platform fashion, or what a competitor did.

## 12. Every Creation Has a Soul
**Belief:** A system without meaning becomes mechanical. The first eleven can be satisfied by a machine — a grid enforced, contrast measured, elements counted and cut. This one cannot. It's the reason the work is worth making, and the only principle whose failure is invisible to every automated check.
**Gate:** Does the work feel like it was made by someone who cared, for someone who matters?
**Fails when:** the answer is a shrug. A shrug is a defect.
